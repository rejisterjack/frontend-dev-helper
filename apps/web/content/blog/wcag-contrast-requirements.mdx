Contrast requirements are the most-failed WCAG success criteria in real audits, mostly because the rules have more nuance than "make it darker." This is a reference for the exact ratios and the cases that trip teams up.

## The ratios

WCAG 2.1 defines two conformance levels for contrast:

| Content type | AA (1.4.3) | AAA (1.4.6) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal text (under 18pt / 24px) | 4.5:1 | 7:1 |
| Large text (18pt+, or 14pt+ bold) | 3:1 | 4.5:1 |
| UI components & graphical objects (1.4.11) | 3:1 | — |

Large text has a lower bar because larger glyphs are legible at lower contrast. Note that 1.4.11 (non-text contrast) only has an AA level — there is no AAA for icons, form borders, or focus indicators.

## What counts as "text"

The ratio applies to text against its background — but "background" means the visual background *behind each glyph*, including gradients and images. A button whose average gradient contrast passes can still fail where the label overlaps the darkest region. Auditing a gradient button means checking the worst-case pair, not the average.

## Common failures automated scanners miss

Scanners reliably catch flat color pairs. They routinely miss:

- **Text over images and gradients** — worst-case region isn't computed.
- **Disabled states** — exempt from 1.4.3, but often exempt-by-accident and unusable.
- **Placeholder text** — frequently lighter than the entered-value style.
- **Icon-only buttons** — 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for the meaningful graphic, and no text ratio to lean on.
- **Focus indicators** — must have 3:1 against adjacent colors (2.4.11 in WCAG 2.2).
- **Charts and data-viz colors** — series colors must be distinguishable from each other and the background.

## Quick math

Contrast is `(L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)` where L1 is the lighter relative luminance. Two things surprise people: pure white on pure yellow fails (1.07:1), and mid-gray on mid-gray can pass AA if the grays are far enough apart. Intuition is a poor substitute for measuring.
